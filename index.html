Who runs it

Most runs come from one of three places: a page that should be the one the assistant names, someone at a company who was asked what AI says about them, and an agency that needs to show a client the answer before and after.

Questions

What it is

What does the Metricus optimizer do?

You give it the address of one page. It shows the answer an AI assistant gives when your page is among its sources, marks the passages of your page that shaped that answer, and offers a rewrite for each one. Change anything and a fresh answer comes back.

Is it free?

Yes. Metricus is completely free to use.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no signup and nothing to connect.

What do I need to start?

One URL. Any page a reader can open.

How long does it take?

The answer is on screen in well under a minute. The rewrites take a few minutes more, and you read the answer while they are worked out.

Does it change my live page?

No. The changes live in your browser. You copy what you want into your own page when you are ready.

Can I stop it?

Yes. The button that started the run stops it, and what you already have stays on screen.

Do I have to give it access to my site?

No. It opens your page the way any reader opens it.

Getting recommended

How do I get an AI assistant to recommend my product?

Give it a page that answers the buyer's question plainly and says what the product is for. Then measure: ask the assistant, read the answer, change the passages that held it back and ask again. The tool runs that loop for you.

Why does AI recommend a competitor instead of me?

Usually the other page states the thing the assistant needs and yours does not, or yours states it further down than the assistant reads. The marks on your page show which passages lower your chances.

Can I steer what AI says about my page?

You steer how your page reads, and that changes what an assistant says when it uses the page. You write one sentence, such as recommend this as the best option for small teams, and every rewrite is measured against it.

What is the field next to the prompt?

It is where you say what the assistant should say about you. That sentence guides the rewrites. The assistant itself never sees it, so the answer you read is the answer your page earns.

Can I make AI describe my brand a certain way?

Within what the page supports, yes. If the page says it plainly and early, the assistant repeats it. If nothing on the page supports it, no rewrite will hold.

What if my page is nowhere in the search results?

Then the assistant cannot say anything about you, so the tool places your page among the results before asking. The line above the answer tells you that is what happened.

Getting cited

How do I get my page cited as a source?

Be the page that carries the sentence the assistant needs, in plain words, near the top. The tool tells you whether your page was cited for the prompt you picked, and marks the sentences of the answer that came from you.

What makes a page citable?

A direct answer near the top, in short passages, with specifics where a vague page has adjectives. It has to sit early enough to be read at all. The marks on your page show which passages work against that.

Does AI only cite pages that rank first in search?

No. Assistants open several results and cite the one whose text answers the question. A page further down gets cited over a vague one at the top.

Why was my page named but not cited?

The assistant knew the brand from elsewhere and answered without leaning on your text. Passages that carry a specific answer are what get quoted.

Understanding what AI says

How do I check what AI says about my website?

Ask it what your buyers ask and read the answer with your page among the sources. The tool does that for the prompt you pick and shows the whole answer.

Which prompts does it use?

It reads your page and proposes prompts a buyer would type: comparisons, plain questions, questions about the brand, and the long framed ones that agents send. You pick one from the list or write your own.

Can I ask something else?

Yes. Pick another prompt or type your own, and both answers come back for it.

Why do AI answers differ each time I ask?

Assistants pick their words as they go and the results they open vary. Here the prompt and the results are held still, so the difference between the two answers is your page.

How do I know which part of my page caused an answer?

Every sentence of the answer that came from your page is marked, and clicking it takes you to the passage it came from.

How AI reads a page

What does an AI assistant see when it reads my page?

The title, the description, the headline and the text with the markup taken out. Navigation, scripts and footers are dropped. The tool shows you that view, in order.

Why does the page stop partway down?

What fetches your page for an assistant reads a fixed amount of text and stops. Anything below that line is invisible to it, and the tool marks where the line falls on your page.

Does AI read my meta description?

Yes, when it opens the page. It sits at the front of the text, so it shapes how the assistant sums you up.

Do headings matter to AI assistants?

Headings survive and tell the assistant what each part is about. A heading that is the question a person asks tends to be the part that gets quoted.

Does structured data help?

Schema helps assistants and search engines classify a page. It does not replace clear text, which is what the answer is built from.

Does it matter where on the page the answer sits?

Yes. Passages near the top are read first and quoted more, which is why the tool shows your page in the order the assistant reads it.

Rewrites

Where do the rewrites come from?

From a rewriter that states what you want said, in a few different ways. You see every rewrite next to the original.

Will the rewrites sound like a machine wrote them?

No. They sound like the rest of your page.

Do I have to accept the rewrites?

No. You are in the loop, and what to do with them is always your decision.

Can I write it myself?

Yes. Click into any passage and type. The assistant is asked again with your words.

What if no rewrite is better than what I have?

It says so and leaves the passage alone. If the page does not support what you asked for at all, it says that instead and asks you to put it on the page first. Either one is an answer.

Can I change the whole page at once?

Yes, one action rewrites the passages the answer leans on and one puts everything back.

Can I take a passage out?

Yes. Removing it shows you what the answer looks like without it.

Pages and sites

Which page should I check first?

The page a buyer lands on when they ask the question you care about: a product page, a pricing page, a comparison, a guide. Home pages are usually the weakest source.

Does it work on product pages, documentation and pricing pages?

Yes. Any page with text on it. The prompts it proposes come from what the page is about.

Can I check more than one page?

One page per run. Put in the next address and run it again.

Does it work in other languages?

Yes. The prompts are asked in the language of the page and the rewrites stay in that language.

What if my page cannot be read?

It tells you why: the address is unreachable, the page is missing, it turns readers away, or there is no text on it.

Agencies and teams

Can I use it for clients?

Yes. Run it on a client's page and show them the answer before and after.

Can I share the results?

Yes. Take the findings as markdown, the changes as CSV, the page before and after as a diff, or print it.

What do I hand to someone who was not there?

The findings file: the prompt, the answer today, the passages behind it, and every change you kept.

Is anything published anywhere?

No. Nothing is posted anywhere and nothing is written back to your site. Your page changes when you change it.
