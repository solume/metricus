AI Visibility Monitoring vs a Check You Run Yourself on One Page

Metricus Research · March 31, 2026 · 10 min read

AI visibility tools fall into two categories: subscription monitoring dashboards that track how often your brand is mentioned over time, and a check you run yourself on one page that shows the answer an AI assistant gives about that page and what on the page caused it. This guide compares the two on cost, depth and fit, so you can decide which one matches how your team works.

What AI visibility tools actually do

The AI visibility market has split into two product models, and the difference matters more than most buyers realize. One model sells ongoing dashboard access on a monthly subscription. The other is a tool you run yourself on one page, with no subscription. They solve different problems, serve different workflows, and cost very different amounts over time.

Monitoring tools (ongoing subscription)

Subscription monitoring tools track how your brand appears in AI responses over time via dashboards and alerts. You pick prompts, and the tool runs them against AI platforms on a regular cadence. The main players: Otterly AI ($29–$489/month), Scrunch AI (~$300/month), Peec AI (EUR 89+/month). These tools are useful for teams running active AI optimization campaigns who need weekly feedback loops.

A check you run yourself on one page (no subscription)

Metricus takes the other approach with a tool you run yourself on one page. Put in a URL and you see the answer an AI assistant gives about that page, the passages on the page that answer came from, marked where they sit, and a rewrite for each one. Change anything and the assistant is asked again. You leave with the changes in a file. It is free, with no signup and no credit card.

When each approach fits

Monitoring subscriptions make sense when you are running active AI optimization campaigns and need weekly feedback, when your competitive landscape changes rapidly, or when you have a dedicated team member interpreting dashboard data. A check you run yourself on one page fits a first baseline, a quarterly review, agency client onboarding, a competitive pitch, and any team that checks AI visibility less than weekly.

Most brands do better to run their key pages through the tool first and establish a baseline before deciding whether ongoing monitoring is worth the money. Without knowing where you stand, you cannot tell whether a monitoring subscription will show you anything you can act on.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between AI visibility monitoring and a check you run yourself?

Monitoring tools track how often your brand appears across a fixed set of prompts, on a schedule, through dashboards and alerts on a monthly subscription. You get trend data and can watch scores change over time. A check you run yourself on one page shows the answer an AI assistant gives about that page, the passages the answer came from, and a rewrite for each one. Monitoring answers what changed. A check on one page answers what that page makes the assistant say and what to change on it.

How much does AI visibility monitoring cost per year?

Subscription monitoring tools typically cost $348 to $6,000+ per year depending on the plan and provider. Otterly AI ranges from $29/month (Lite) to $489/month (Premium). Scrunch AI runs about $300/month. Peec AI starts at EUR 89/month. By comparison, a check you run yourself on one page is free, so running it quarterly costs nothing.

Should I start with monitoring or a check on one page?

Start with a check on one page. You need a baseline before monitoring makes sense. It tells you what an AI assistant says about your key pages, what it gets wrong, and what to change first. Once you have made those changes, you can judge whether ongoing monitoring adds enough to justify the subscription.

How often should I check AI visibility?

Quarterly is enough for most brands. That cadence gives AI systems time to pick up the changes you made between checks. Check again after major events: a rebrand, a product launch, a large content push, or a competitive shift. Brands in fast-moving categories with aggressive competitors may benefit from monthly checks or a monitoring tool.

When should I switch from checks to monitoring?

Consider switching when three conditions are met: you are actively running AI optimization campaigns and need weekly feedback on what is working, your competitive landscape changes fast enough that quarterly checks miss important shifts, and you have a team member who can read dashboard data and turn it into action. If you check AI visibility less than weekly, a check you run yourself stays the cheaper option.

Can agencies use a check on one page instead of monitoring subscriptions?

Yes, and many do. Running a client's key pages through the tool works for onboarding (showing on day one what an AI assistant says about their pages), competitive pitches (showing a prospect what their pages cause), and quarterly business reviews. It avoids accumulating monthly subscriptions across a client portfolio. For retained clients with active AI optimization campaigns, some agencies add monitoring for those accounts and use the tool for the rest.
