AI Visibility Monitoring vs a Single Check

Metricus Research · March 31, 2026 · 10 min read

AI visibility tools fall into two categories: subscription monitoring dashboards that track how often your brand is mentioned over time, and a single check that shows what AI says about you today. This guide compares the two on cost, depth and fit, so you can decide which one matches how your team works.

What AI visibility tools actually do

The AI visibility market has split into two product models, and the difference matters more than most buyers realize. One model sells ongoing dashboard access on a monthly subscription. The other is a single check you run when you need it, with no subscription. They solve different problems, serve different workflows, and cost very different amounts over time.

Monitoring tools (ongoing subscription)

Subscription monitoring tools track how your brand appears in AI responses over time via dashboards and alerts. You pick prompts, and the tool runs them against AI platforms on a regular cadence. The main players: Otterly AI ($29–$489/month), Scrunch AI (~$300/month), Peec AI (EUR 89+/month). These tools are useful for teams running active AI optimization campaigns who need weekly feedback loops.

A single check (no subscription)

A single check means asking AI the questions your customers ask, in their words, and noting which businesses the answer names and what it says about yours. Done by hand, it costs nothing. It gives you one sample: what one AI platform said to one question at one point in time. That is enough for a baseline, but not for tracking change.

If your team checks AI visibility quarterly, paying $300/month for a dashboard you log into four times per year means paying $900 per check. Checking by hand costs nothing, so there are no wasted months.

When each approach fits

Monitoring subscriptions make sense when you are running active AI optimization campaigns and need weekly feedback, when your competitive landscape changes rapidly, or when you have a dedicated team member interpreting dashboard data. A single check fits a first baseline, a quarterly review, agency client onboarding, a competitive pitch, and any team that checks AI visibility less than weekly.

Most brands do better to check the questions their customers ask first and establish a baseline before deciding whether ongoing monitoring is worth the money. Without knowing where you stand, you cannot tell whether a monitoring subscription will show you anything you can act on.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between AI visibility monitoring and a single check?

Monitoring tools track how often your brand appears across a fixed set of prompts, on a schedule, through dashboards and alerts on a monthly subscription. You get trend data and can watch scores change over time. A single check shows what AI answers today when you ask the questions your customers ask: which businesses it names and what it says about yours. Monitoring answers what changed. A single check answers where you stand today.

How much does AI visibility monitoring cost per year?

Subscription monitoring tools typically cost $348 to $6,000+ per year depending on the plan and provider. Otterly AI ranges from $29/month (Lite) to $489/month (Premium). Scrunch AI runs about $300/month. Peec AI starts at EUR 89/month. By comparison, checking by hand is free, so doing it quarterly costs nothing.

Should I start with monitoring or a single check?

Start with a single check. You need a baseline before monitoring makes sense. It tells you what AI says about your business when customers ask, what it gets wrong, and what to change first. Once you have made those changes, you can judge whether ongoing monitoring adds enough to justify the subscription.

How often should I check AI visibility?

Quarterly is enough for most brands. That cadence gives AI systems time to pick up the changes you made between checks. Check again after major events: a rebrand, a product launch, a large content push, or a competitive shift. Brands in fast-moving categories with aggressive competitors may benefit from monthly checks or a monitoring tool.

When should I switch from checks to monitoring?

Consider switching when three conditions are met: you are actively running AI optimization campaigns and need weekly feedback on what is working, your competitive landscape changes fast enough that quarterly checks miss important shifts, and you have a team member who can read dashboard data and turn it into action. If you check AI visibility less than weekly, checking by hand stays the cheaper option.

Can agencies use single checks instead of monitoring subscriptions?

Yes, and many do. Checking the questions a client's customers ask works for onboarding (showing on day one what AI says about the client), competitive pitches (showing a prospect which businesses AI names instead), and quarterly business reviews. It avoids accumulating monthly subscriptions across a client portfolio. For retained clients with active AI optimization campaigns, some agencies add monitoring for those accounts and use single checks for the rest.
