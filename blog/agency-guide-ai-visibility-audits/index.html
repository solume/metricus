Agency Guide to AI Visibility: Running the Metricus Optimizer on Client Pages

Metricus · Last updated: September 2026

Agencies can run the free Metricus optimizer on any client page. Put in the URL and you see the answer an AI assistant gives about that page, the passages on the page that caused it, and a rewrite for each one. No account, nothing installed, nothing changed on the client's live site. This guide covers what to run, what to show the client, which files you can hand over, and how to scope the work you charge for.

Why Clients Are Asking

The conversation in every client meeting has shifted. Alongside SEO rankings and paid search performance, brands now ask a question most agencies weren't prepared for: "Why doesn't ChatGPT recommend us?" This is not a niche concern from early adopters. A growing share of B2B and B2C buyers now consult AI assistants during purchasing research, and when a brand is absent from those answers, clients notice. Metricus research data shows that brands visible in AI responses capture consideration before a prospect ever reaches a search engine. Agencies that can answer the AI visibility question retain clients who would otherwise look elsewhere for expertise.

Clients do not want another dashboard login. They want to see what an assistant says about their pages, why it says it, and what to change. A run of the optimizer gives you that for one page, and the files it produces are what the client keeps.

What to Run

Start with the pages a buyer reaches while comparing options: the main product or service pages, the pricing page, and the page that says what the company does. Home pages are usually the weakest source, so run them last. Put in each URL and read the answer first. Note where it is wrong, thin, or missing the claim the client cares about most.

Then read the marked passages. The answer traces back to specific sentences on the page, often ones written for a different purpose, such as a headline that never says what the product is. Each marked passage comes with a rewrite. Try the rewrite, or type your own wording, and the assistant is asked again, so you can see whether the answer changed before anything is published. Run one page at a time and keep a list of which pages you ran and what changed on each.

What to Show the Client

Show the answer first. A client recognizes their own page in the assistant's words, and a wrong or vague answer makes the case for the work without a score to argue over. Then show the marked passages beside the answer, so the client sees the cause. Then show the answer after the rewrites. The before and after on the client's own page is the whole presentation.

Present only the rewrites the client can stand behind. A rewrite that claims something the product does not do is worse than the original, so check each one against what the client actually offers before the meeting.

The Files You Can Hand Over

A run ends with files you can pass to the client or to whoever edits the site. Each one comes straight from the tool:

  • A diff of the page before and after, so the developer or editor sees every changed passage in place.
  • A CSV of the changes, one row per change, which drops into a ticket system or a content calendar.
  • The findings as markdown: the prompt, the answer today, the passages behind it, and every change you kept. This is the file for a client write-up.
  • A printed copy of the run as it stands, for a meeting.

Nothing is written back to the client's site. The changes go live when whoever edits the site puts them in, which is the part of the work you control and can bill.

How to Scope the Work You Charge For

The run itself is free, so it is not the thing you bill. Bill the judgment around it: choosing which pages matter, deciding which rewrites fit the client's claims and voice, getting them approved, publishing them on the live site, and running each page again afterwards to confirm the answer changed. Quote per page or per batch of pages, and write those steps into the scope so the client knows what the fee covers.

Compare that with per-client subscriptions to AI monitoring dashboards, which run $189 to $300 per client per month, need analyst time to interpret, and still have to be turned into something the client can act on. For an agency with ten clients, that is $22,000 to $36,000 a year in tooling before any work is done. Monitoring has its place when a client wants mentions tracked over time. Fixing pages is a different job. For that job the tool costs nothing, so the fee is for your work, and the client can see what the fee bought.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do agencies use the Metricus optimizer with clients?

Run it on each page the engagement covers, show the client the answer an AI assistant gives about the page and the passages that caused it, agree on the rewrites, and hand over the changes as files from the tool. There is no account and nothing to onboard.

No. The optimizer runs in the browser and shows the answer, the marked passages, and the rewrites. Publishing the rewrites needs whoever already edits the client's site, and nothing else.

No. The Metricus optimizer is free, with no signup, no credit card, no monthly fee, and no contract. Run it on as many client pages as you need.
